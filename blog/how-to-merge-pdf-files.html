How to Merge PDF Files on Any Device (Free Methods)

By FreeToolsHub Editorial Team · Published January 15, 2025 · 5 min read

Sending five separate PDF attachments in an email or uploading individual scanned pages one by one to a portal is frustrating for both you and the recipient. Combining those files into a single, cleanly ordered PDF keeps everything together—and you do not need to pay for Adobe Acrobat Pro or install bloated desktop software to do it. Below are four reliable, free ways to merge PDFs on Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS.

Why Merge PDFs?

Consolidating documents into a single PDF is one of the most common office and academic tasks:

  • Job Applications & Portfolios: Combine your resume, cover letter, and certifications into one file so recruiters never miss a page.
  • Expense Reports & Invoices: Merge monthly receipts with a summary sheet for faster accounting approval.
  • Client Proposals & Contracts: Bundle a project scope, pricing schedule, and signed terms into a single archival document.
  • University Assignments: Submit multi-part lab reports or scanned handwritten derivations as one continuous PDF.

Method 2: Windows Built-in Print to PDF

Windows 10 and Windows 11 do not include a native "merge PDF" button in File Explorer, but you can combine files offline using the built-in Microsoft Print to PDF driver or by using a browser:

  1. If you want to combine images or Word documents with a PDF, open each file, choose Print, and select Microsoft Print to PDF to standardize them as PDFs.
  2. To merge those PDFs cleanly without rasterizing vector text, open them in a browser-based client-side merger like FreeToolsHub rather than re-printing (re-printing a PDF can turn selectable text into flat images).

Method 3: macOS Preview

Every Mac ships with the Preview app, which has built-in PDF merging capabilities:

  1. Double-click your first PDF to open it in Preview.
  2. Click View → Thumbnails in the top menu bar so the page sidebar appears.
  3. Drag your second PDF file from Finder directly onto the thumbnail sidebar where you want the new pages inserted.
  4. Press Cmd + S (or choose File → Export as PDF) to save the combined document.

macOS Preview works well for quick desktop edits, though it occasionally inflates file sizes when saving scanned documents.

Method 4: Mobile Apps (Android & iOS)

On smartphones, you have two clean options without downloading ad-heavy PDF apps:

  • iPhone & iPad (Files App): Open the built-in Files app, tap the three-dot menu (…), choose Select, tap two or more PDFs in order, tap More (… ) in the bottom-right corner, and choose Create PDF.
  • Android (Browser): Android does not include a universal native merger in its default file manager. Instead of installing a third-party app full of subscriptions, open FreeToolsHub PDF Merge in Chrome, pick your PDFs from the Downloads folder, and save the merged file locally.

What to Watch Out For

  • Encrypted or Password-Protected PDFs: Bank statements and tax forms are sometimes locked with an owner or user password. Client-side mergers cannot bypass encryption, so unlock the PDF first before merging.
  • Very Large Files on Mobile: Because browser-based merging runs in your device's RAM, combining ten 50 MB scanned PDFs on an older phone may feel sluggish. On desktop browsers, files up to 100+ MB merge smoothly.
  • Bookmarks & Interactive Forms: Merging page contents preserves vector text, links, and layout, though interactive table-of-contents bookmarks from separate files are not always carried over automatically.
  • Privacy on Public Websites: Never upload confidential IDs, contracts, or medical records to server-based PDF websites unless you have verified their data-retention policy. Read our Online Tool Privacy Guide to learn how to check.
